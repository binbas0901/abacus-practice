# Replace the Soroban Class in Three Phases

**Build a static, offline-capable iPad web app in three phases: first a faithful drop-in for the paid portal's two drills (visual mitorizan and listening yomiagezan) driven by a rule-aware problem generator; second, a teaching layer that unlocks complement rules one at a time behind an accuracy gate and gives the parent a dashboard and script; third, a small daily anzan side-drill using the official flash-anzan ladder's easiest settings.** The research supports this ordering because every curriculum examined (Kodomo Soroban Center, Soroban Dojo, Kani Math, Japanese kyu exams) follows the same skeleton — direct moves, then 5-complements ("small friends"), then 10-complements ("big friends"), then combined moves, then multi-digit columns, with multiplication arriving only around Japanese 9-kyu ([KSC](https://kodomo-soroban-center.com/en/bai-tap-soroban/); [Coeteco](https://coeteco.jp/articles/12704)) — so a 6-year-old on the "10 family" is squarely mid-ladder with months of add/subtract work ahead before anything new is needed. The single most valuable engineering decision is the step classifier: a 10x9 lookup that labels every single-rod bead move as direct, five-complement, ten-complement or combined, including recursive carries and borrows, so the generator can produce "10-family only" sets that the commercial generators cannot ([Dyalog soroban rules](https://dfns.dyalog.com/eval_s_soroban.htm); [Van Cleave](https://joevancleave.blogspot.com/2022/04/the-soroban-abacus-addition-method.html?m=1)). For audio, pre-recorded number clips scheduled with the Web Audio clock beat `speechSynthesis`, which on iPadOS exposes only low-tier pre-installed voices and has a long history of silent regressions ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/723503); [textintoaudio](https://textintoaudio-com.pages.dev/browser-support.html)). Pedagogically, the evidence says keep sessions to about 10 minutes daily, gate progression on roughly 80% accuracy rather than streaks, and treat anzan as a one-minute garnish until bead movements are automatic ([Bets Abacus](https://betsabacus.blogspot.com/2016/11/home-practice-by-students-parents-role.html); [KSC](https://kodomo-soroban-center.com/en/bai-tap-soroban/); [Art of Memory](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/anzan-flash-method/30070)). Two important gaps remain and are flagged throughout: the exact digits-per-row tables for Japanese 10-kyu to 7-kyu mitorizan were not retrievable from official sources, and no source publishes a per-number interval ladder for listening drills, so the 10-second starting point must be tuned empirically.

## She sits at KSC Level 1A, pre-9-kyu, with months of add/subtract still ahead

Every sequenced curriculum found agrees on the order of skills, and the agreement is strong enough to build a skill tree on. Soroban Dojo runs L0 (parts, zero position, place value) to L1 (setting numbers, one-rod addition and subtraction) to L2 (complements to five, complements to ten, "what to do when direct movement is blocked") to L3 (multi-rod fluency with 2- and 3-digit mixed drills) before multiplication at L4 and anzan at L5 ([Soroban Dojo curriculum](https://dmoliveira.github.io/soroban-dojo/curriculum)). Kodomo Soroban Center's 32-sheet progression makes the same order concrete: Sheet 01 is direct addition of single digits with three numbers per problem and no exchanges; Sheet 05 introduces the **5-friend rule with no carrying to tens**; Sheet 06 is the **10-friend rule with carrying, 30 problems of 4 numbers each, every problem carrying at least once**; Sheet 08 adds 5-friend and 10-friend subtraction; Sheet 09 is mitorizan with 2-digit numbers, 5 numbers per problem, "mixing all rules, including combined ones"; Sheet 10 moves to 3-digit, 5 rows, "in exam format"; and only at Sheets 11 and 12 do 2-digit by 1-digit multiplication and single-digit division appear ([KSC](https://kodomo-soroban-center.com/en/bai-tap-soroban/)). Kani Math lists the same ladder in its own words — counting to 99, small friends using pairs 1+4 and 2+3, big friends where "you add ten on the rod to the left and take the difference off the current rod," then anzan, then multiplication "starts at Level 7" ([Kani Math](https://www.kani-math.com/practice)).

The vocabulary differs by school but the content does not. "Small friends" are the two pairs that make five (1 and 4, 2 and 3); "big friends" are the pairs that make ten; US classroom flashcard decks use exactly this language, placing "Addition With Big Friends, using 10 − x" at Lesson 4 (grade 2) ([Brainscape](https://www.brainscape.com/flashcards/abacus-lesson-4-addition-with-big-friend-19182292/packs/22960196)). Franchise material enumerates every big-friend formula separately — +9 = −1 +10 through +1 = −9 +10 for addition, −9 = +1 −10 through −1 = +9 −10 for subtraction — and defines "mixed complements" as formulas combining addition and subtraction via big friends "when direct bead movement isn't possible" ([AbacusTrainer](https://www.abacustrainer.com/short-videos/mixed-complements-in-abacus-big-friends-addition-subtraction-explained-for-kids/C_RrKOo6eyk)). The literal phrase "10 family" did not appear in any fetched source; it is school-specific vocabulary for the same concept, and the app should simply adopt her class's words. One ordering inside the 10-complement block is unresolved: franchise videos list +9 first and descend, Soroban Dojo lists pairs ascending (1 and 9, 2 and 8...), and no source argued for one pedagogical order over the other ([Soroban Dojo L2](https://dmoliveira.github.io/soroban-dojo/lessons/l2/complements-to-ten)). Soroban Dojo does flag the two beginner errors that matter for app design: "remembering a pair in only one direction" and "treating complements as pure memorization rather than as helpers for moving beads," and it recommends saying both numbers of each pair aloud ([Soroban Dojo complement ladders](https://dmoliveira.github.io/soroban-dojo/lessons/l2/complement-ladders)).

The combined case deserves its own tree node because it is intrinsically harder. When adding 6 to a rod showing 8, the big-friend move is "+10 on the left, −4 here," and that −4 on a rod showing 8 is direct; but adding 7 to a rod showing 6 requires "−3 here" on a rod whose earth beads show only 1, so the −3 itself needs a small-friend exchange (remove the five bead, push up two). KSC introduces these "combined" cases only at Sheet 09, after both plain big-friend sheets ([KSC](https://kodomo-soroban-center.com/en/bai-tap-soroban/)). The classifier in a later section enumerates exactly which 10 of the 90 addition cells and which 10 of the 90 subtraction cells are combined.

On exam standards, the Japanese federations provide the de facto level definitions, and the beginner shape is modest. Nisshuren (日珠連, run with the Chamber of Commerce) tests 10-kyu and 9-kyu with **10 mitorizan problems plus 10 multiplication problems in 20 minutes**, adding division from 8-kyu; pass marks are **60/200 at 10-kyu** (a deliberately low bar), 120/200 at 9–7-kyu, and 210/300 at 6–4-kyu where the paper grows to 10 mitorizan, 20 multiplication and 20 division in 30 minutes ([Coeteco 12704](https://coeteco.jp/articles/12704); [Passnavi](https://passnavi.obunsha.co.jp/shinro/license-job/business-0226/); [Tosu CCI PDF](https://www.tosucci.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/07syuzan.pdf)). Zenshuren (全珠連) runs lower, to 15-kyu, where **10–15-kyu are mitorizan only, 7 minutes per subject**, with a 100/150 per-subject pass at 8–4-kyu ([Coeteco 12704](https://coeteco.jp/articles/12704)). Zenshuren's anzan exam gives 3 minutes per subject, 20 problems at 5 points each, pass at 70; its 8th–10th anzan grades contain only addition/subtraction, and a Zenshuren teacher reports multiplication enters at abacus Grade 9 and division at Grade 8 ([note.com Yoshiro-sensei](https://note.com/20170723/n/n20570a678d7b?hl=en)). The Japanese pace norm for a child starting in April of first grade with twice-weekly class and no homework is 9–8-kyu by the end of the first year, with multiplication "around January of 1st grade" ([Coeteco 12704](https://coeteco.jp/articles/12704)). A 6-year-old on 10-complements after some months is on that track.

**Gap:** the digits-per-number and rows-per-problem (口数) tables for Nisshuren 10–7-kyu and Zenshuren 15–6-kyu mitorizan could not be retrieved; the official pages at shuzan.jp and soroban.or.jp were not fetchable and the chamber PDFs only cover 1–6-kyu scoring. Do not hard-code "9-kyu = N digits x M rows" until confirmed from a current 検定問題集. The safe proxy is the KSC shape (1-digit x 3–4 rows, then 2-digit x 5, then 3-digit x 5).

## Real programs score by count-correct and gate at 60–80%, never by streaks

The portal's visual mode — all signed numbers of a problem shown at once as a column, answer typed, ten per set — is the standard worksheet shape, not a vendor quirk. Japanese kyu papers are 10 mitorizan problems scored purely by number correct ([Coeteco 12704](https://coeteco.jp/articles/12704)); the All-Japan Correspondence Soroban Contest sheet uses signed rows (negative values) under a 5-minute limit, scored at 10 points per correct answer ([soroban.or.jp contest PDF](https://www.soroban.or.jp/soroban-wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/fecbdeb3702930d6d3c2cafac400a73a.pdf)); and the mentalcalculation.org "Soroban Exam" simulator grades each session as a kyu/dan from the count correct, lists missed questions with submitted versus correct answers, and lets the user format add/sub horizontally or vertically with configurable font size and spacing ([mentalcalculation.org](https://mentalcalculation.org/soroban-rating-simulator)). The Japanese そろばん問題集 app shows drill problems in worksheet format, lets the student use an on-screen or physical abacus, and grades typed answers, covering all Zenshuren problems from 10-kyu to 1-kyu ([App Store そろばん問題集](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/%E3%81%9D%E3%82%8D%E3%81%B0%E3%82%93%E5%95%8F%E9%A1%8C%E9%9B%86/id1527023159?l=ko)). The right default for Phase 1 is therefore the portal's exact shape: 10 problems, 4 signed single-digit numbers stacked, numeric keypad entry, count-correct scoring, with the missed-problem list exposed afterward.

Listening drills have a codified Japanese script but no published interval ladder. The phrasing is "ねがいましては" to open, "円なり" after each non-final number, "円では" after the last, "引いては" when switching to subtraction and "加えて" when returning to addition ([zenn.dev](https://zenn.dev/dzonesasaki/articles/12ef0024eda8f9)). The one implemented open-source web trainer found uses relative speech rates of **0.5, 0.75 and 1.0** for "very slow / slow / normal," speaks the answer **3 seconds after the reading ends**, defaults to 2-digit numbers and 5 terms, and is built on the Web Speech API with a ja-JP voice ([zenn.dev](https://zenn.dev/dzonesasaki/articles/12ef0024eda8f9)). Non-Japanese franchise programs scale dictation by row count instead: the UCMAS-style "Dictation App" uses single or double digits with 10/20/30/40 rows by level, and was built because the "daily home dictation that a parent or elder usually reads aloud" is often skipped, which "slows progress and leads to dropouts" ([Dictation App](https://sites.google.com/view/dictationapp/home)). Abacutor added the ability to replay oral-dictation problems for exam review ([App Store Abacutor](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/abacutor/id1469765610)). Competition yomiagezan escalates from 3–5 digit addition-only at problem 1 to 7–16 digit add/subtract by problem 15, read once to the room, but states no tempo ([Iwamizawa rulebook PDF](https://www.city.iwamizawa.hokkaido.jp/material/files/group/89/04_zendoutaikai_youkou.pdf)). **Gap:** no source corroborates or places the daughter's ~10-second interval on any ladder, and no written English phrasing specification ("plus seven... minus three... equals") was found. The app must expose the inter-number gap in seconds as a first-class slider and let the parent reduce it empirically.

Flash anzan is the best-documented mode and gives the app a published ladder to borrow. The official Japan Flash Anzan Association app runs **120 levels from 1 digit x 2 numbers at 3.0 seconds per number to 3 digits x 15 numbers at 1.2 seconds**, with typed answers and spoken correctness ([App Store フラッシュ暗算(公式)](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/%E3%83%95%E3%83%A9%E3%83%83%E3%82%B7%E3%83%A5%E6%9A%97%E7%AE%97-%E5%85%AC%E5%BC%8F/id421826349)). A competing app aligned to the same exam puts **23-kyu at 2 numbers, max 9, 2.00 s interval**, 初段 at 15 numbers of 2 digits at 0.67 s, and 20-dan at 15 numbers of 3 digits at 0.10 s; its promotion test is sets of 5 problems needing 20 total correct, and only "Challenge" runs count toward scores ([app-liv.jp](https://app-liv.jp/5354896/)). Soroban66 adds an adjustable "lighting time ratio" (how much of each slot the number is lit versus blank) and 0.01 s increments ([App Store Soroban66](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1661734047)). The ladder's structure is itself a lesson: it adds count and digits long before it drops below about one second per number, so the app should shorten time before adding numbers, and add numbers before adding digits.

Gating in every real program is accuracy over a fixed set, not streaks. The only explicit worksheet pass mark found is KSC's **"80% correct in a reasonable time,"** the same standard it uses for its tests, with timed-grid targets of 3 minutes for 100 additions and 4 minutes for 100 subtractions ([KSC](https://kodomo-soroban-center.com/en/bai-tap-soroban/)). Exam pass bands run from 30% at Nisshuren 10-kyu through 67% (Zenshuren 100/150) to 70% (Nisshuren 210/300) ([Coeteco 12704](https://coeteco.jp/articles/12704)). No federation or franchise publishes a "move on at X% in Y seconds" rule; instructor practice is judgment plus exam results. No evidence of streak-based gating was found in any real program. Reporting in the apps found is aimed at teachers — Abacutor's "Sensei" sees classwork in real time and assigns homework ([App Store Abacutor](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/abacutor/id1469765610)); the Dictation App records every session with weekly, monthly and per-level dashboards linked to a centre ([Dictation App](https://sites.google.com/view/dictationapp/home)) — which is exactly the shape a parent dashboard should copy.

The complaint record tells the app what not to be. Reviewers of the official flash-anzan app want iPad support and 4-digit problems, say levels do not match the association's, and note the app was last updated in 2017 ([App Store 公式](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/%E3%83%95%E3%83%A9%E3%83%83%E3%82%B7%E3%83%A5%E6%9A%97%E7%AE%97-%E5%85%AC%E5%BC%8F/id421826349)). The そろばん式 flash app paywalls custom difficulty and ad removal, lacks multiplication and division, and does not count practice runs ([app-liv.jp](https://app-liv.jp/5354896/)). Soroban Drill lacks landscape mode, making multi-digit work impractical ([App Store Soroban Drill](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/soroban-drill/id1348246229)). Mental Abacus ($8.99) is called "really boring" with no games, and ships its practice problems in a separate paid app ([App Store Mental Abacus](https://apps.apple.com/app/id489127101)). A franchise app's verification code failed for a child, and a parent was stressed because the teacher awarded trophies by speed ranking ([App Store UCMAS CY GR](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ucmas-cy-gr/id1491179435?ls=1)). The simulator that does everything right is "not mobile-compatible" ([mentalcalculation.org](https://mentalcalculation.org/soroban-rating-simulator)). A home-built app wins by being landscape-first on iPad, free of paywalls on every slider, labelled against a published ladder, and free of competitive leaderboards.

## Ten minutes daily, parent reads aloud, abacus is the authority

Practitioner guidance on dose is consistent and modest. An abacus-school teacher tells parents to have children "practice every day ten minutes," treats home practice as one-third of total practice per level, and warns that cramming six days of practice into a weekend "leads to many mistakes and can push children back to conventional calculation methods" ([Bets Abacus](https://betsabacus.blogspot.com/2016/11/home-practice-by-students-parents-role.html)). Soroban Dojo's children's path prescribes "5–10 minutes per session" and "a short happy finish is better than a long tired session" ([Soroban Dojo children path](https://dmoliveira.github.io/soroban-dojo/paths/children)). A homeschool parent recommends "verbal practice every day for 5–10 minutes" where the parent reads problems aloud ([Well-Trained Mind](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/493238-anyone-taught-their-kids-anzan-mental-sorobanabacus-math-and-vedic-math/)). Smartick caps children at one 15-minute session a day, asserting (without cited studies) that this is the attention ceiling for its age group ([Smartick](https://support.smartickmethod.com/en/articles/597945-why-just-15-minutes-a-day)). For contrast, the research-grade dose was 3 hours a week over three years ([Barner et al. 2016](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0691p7tp/qt0691p7tp.pdf?t=o6eejp)); home practice will be far below that, so expect slower-than-class progress. **Recommended default (inference): one 10-minute session, 6 days a week, hard-stopped by the app with a positive summary screen.**

The parent technique checklist can be assembled from four sources. The three standard Japanese finger rules are: thumb raises a one-bead, index finger lowers a one-bead, index finger moves the five-bead both ways; a Japanese parenting guide adds that if a class uses a different method, ask why, and that most classes use 23-rod abacuses because smaller ones limit later progress ([Coeteco 12692](https://coeteco.jp/articles/12692)). The Nuffield soroban poster states "to show 7, you pinch with the finger and thumb," and multi-digit numbers are set "left to right" ([Nuffield poster PDF](https://www.nuffieldfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/poster1.pdf)); KSC's Sheet 03 notes "tens are handled before units" ([KSC](https://kodomo-soroban-center.com/en/bai-tap-soroban/)). Adult learners stress that "the finger movements on each rod can be done in one go with 2 fingers max" and to "use one fixed movement for each case" ([Art of Memory](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/soroban-and-anzan/33294); [Art of Memory flash method](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/anzan-flash-method/30070)). Teachers relayed by parents insist students should move beads rather than solve with conventional methods, and that soroban is "an add-on, not a full curriculum" ([Well-Trained Mind](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/493238-anyone-taught-their-kids-anzan-mental-sorobanabacus-math-and-vedic-math/)). Soroban Dojo recommends reading the complement pair aloud first ("7 needs 3 more to reach 10") before moving beads ([Soroban Dojo complements to ten](https://dmoliveira.github.io/soroban-dojo/lessons/l2/complements-to-ten)). Synthesized into a parent-mode card: frame flat and still; thumb up, index down, index for the five-bead; set 6–9 in one pinch, never by counting; work left to right; say the friend before the move; the beads are the answer, never arrange them to match a mental answer; one new idea per session; stop while happy. Warning signs of a shaky foundation are whispered counting, touching beads to count them, hesitating over which finger, and answering from memory while the abacus shows a different state (inference from the sources above; no source gave a systematic error list).

Readiness to advance is defined operationally as automaticity. A Japanese-affiliated school ends each level with "a timed pre-test" ([Well-Trained Mind](https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/571662-abacus-soroban-what-have-you-tried/)); adult learners say "learn the soroban until the finger movements become automatic" ([Art of Memory](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/soroban-and-anzan/33294)); timed drills helped one perfectionist child "not freak out anymore if he makes a mistake" ([Davidson Gifted](https://giftedissues.davidsongifted.org/bb/ubbthreads.php/topics/174407.html)). Patience is warranted at this age: in the one-year US classroom RCT, first-graders "struggled to achieve abacus expertise" while second-graders were more successful ([Barner et al. 2018](https://www.psycharchives.org/handle/20.500.12034/1271)).

The anzan transition should be gradual and small. Barner's RCT curriculum had "the first year focused mainly on the physical abacus, with more emphasis on mental computation in later years," and found mental-abacus users limited to about three or four columns ([Barner et al. 2016](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0691p7tp/qt0691p7tp.pdf?t=o6eejp)); Frank and Barner showed users could not imagine more than about three columns, that performance dropped sharply on four-digit addition, and that the representation is visual and stored per column ([Phys.org on Frank & Barner](https://phys.org/news/2011-08-stanford-explores-language.html)). The practitioner transition recipe is: do problems physically, then replay the same bead movements in the mind several times; mentally set every number encountered; drill 1+1 through 9+9 systematically with one fixed movement per case; begin flash work with one-digit numbers, where about one second per one-digit addition is comfortable, then two digits ([Art of Memory flash method](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/anzan-flash-method/30070)). Eyes-closed answers, finger movements in the air, and bead-to-number flashcards stabilize the image; "number interference" (digits replacing the bead picture) is a common obstacle, and a child who says three digits "is too hard" is behaving normally ([Art of Memory](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/soroban-and-anzan/33294)). A home guide warns against "rushing into mental math before mastering the physical abacus" ([WuKong](https://wukongsch.com/blog/?p=23278)).

The evidence base also sets expectations honestly. The three-year Indian RCT (204 children starting grade 2, 3 hours a week) found year-3 arithmetic gains of **d = .60** on in-house tests but only d = .24 on standardized calculation, no reliable transfer to spatial working memory, Raven's or mental rotation, and gains concentrated in children with higher baseline spatial working memory ([Barner et al. 2016](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0691p7tp/qt0691p7tp.pdf?t=o6eejp); [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/teaching-children-ancient-mental-abacus-technique-boosted-their-maths-abilities)). The one-year US trial found "neither age group showed a significant advantage" ([Barner et al. 2018](https://www.psycharchives.org/handle/20.500.12034/1271)). The app should promise arithmetic fluency and number sense on a multi-year horizon, not brain training.

## Pre-recorded clips on the Web Audio clock beat speechSynthesis on iPad

The listening drill is the technically hardest requirement, and the research points firmly toward pre-recorded audio. `speechSynthesis` on iPadOS is usable but fragile: the first `speak()` must happen synchronously inside a tap handler or it fails with `not-allowed`, and mobile browsers "frequently stop speech when the screen locks or the tab is hidden" ([textintoaudio](https://textintoaudio-com.pages.dev/browser-support.html)). An Apple engineer stated in 2023 that "only the pre-installed voices are available. Optionally downloadable voices are not available," and the exposed set has fluctuated across iOS 16–18, at one point leaving only the low-quality Eloquence novelty voices on an iPad ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/723503)). Readium's notes add that "the highest-quality voices, those used by Siri, are not available through the Web Speech API," that all voices report `default: true`, that some appear twice, and that `voiceURI` is not a stable identifier ([Readium WebSpeech doc](https://readium.org/speech/docs/WebSpeech.html)). End events did not fire after `cancel()` in a 2020 regression ([WebKit bug 211776](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=211776)), and Safari 26.0 (September 2025) was still fixing "pending utterances do not receive an error event when speech synthesis is cancelled" ([Safari 26.0 release notes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/safari-release-notes/safari-26-release-notes.md)). Null-voice utterances can produce no speech at all on some regions ([WebKit bug 243055](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=243055)). No source measured `onend` jitter on iOS, so precise N-second pacing cannot be trusted to it.

Web Audio solves the timing problem outright. `audioContext.currentTime` is the audio-hardware clock, `start(when)` aligns to individual samples, and a lookahead scheduler (a ~25 ms timer scheduling ~100 ms ahead) survives main-thread delays that would wreck `setTimeout` pacing ([web.dev "A tale of two clocks"](https://web.dev/audio-scheduling/)). The costs are known: a user gesture is still required before any audio plays; the iPhone mute switch silences Web Audio but not `<audio>` elements, which the `unmute-ios-audio` trick works around by playing a silent sound through both on first interaction ([feross/unmute-ios-audio](https://github.com/feross/unmute-ios-audio)); and on iOS 17 the AudioContext is not consistently resumed when returning to the foreground, with `suspend()` then `resume()` as the workaround ([WebKit bug 263627](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=263627)). Clip inventory is small: English 0–999 stitches from about 30 word clips, or records naturally as 1,000 whole-number clips totalling a few megabytes, with a sensible middle path of 0–99 individually plus "X hundred" variants (about 118 clips) — these counts are arithmetic, not sourced. **Recommendation: record the parent's own voice** for 0–99 plus the operator words ("plus," "minus," "equals," and her class's opening phrase), stitch hundreds, and keep `speechSynthesis` only as a fallback and as the engine for the Phase 2 hint narration where timing does not matter.

Storage and offline are no longer concerns. Since iOS 17, origin quota is up to 60% of disk for Safari and for Home Screen web apps, covering IndexedDB, Cache API and service workers, and `navigator.storage.persist()` is granted by heuristics with "opened as a Home Screen Web App" cited as an example ([WebKit storage policy](https://webkit.org/?p=14403)). WebKit implemented "an explicit exception for the first-party domain of home screen web applications" from ITP's data removal, with that data isolated from Safari ([WebKit CNAME blog](https://webkit.org/blog/11338/cname-cloaking-and-bounce-tracking-defense/)); MDN confirms Home Screen apps use the browser-app quota and that Safari's seven-day eviction targets script-created data of origins without recent interaction ([MDN storage quotas](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria)). A widely circulated claim that Safari storage is "capped at 50MB" with no home-screen exemption ([MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide)) contradicts WebKit's own 2023 policy and should be treated as legacy. Storage remains best-effort under disk pressure, so ship an "Export progress" JSON via Web Share and never store progress in JavaScript-set cookies, which are capped at seven days regardless ([WebKit bug 237350](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237350)). Service workers, Cache Storage and offline have worked in installed web apps since iOS 11.3; Background Sync is absent; since Safari 26 any website can be installed as a web app even without a manifest; manifest `orientation`, `background_color` and `fullscreen` are still ignored ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)). Screen Wake Lock works in Home Screen web apps since iPadOS 18.4, which matters because speech stops on screen lock ([WebKit Safari 18.4](https://webkit.org/?p=16574)).

Guided Access is the system-level child lock and complements an in-app parent gate. From Settings > Accessibility it locks the iPad to one app (including an installed web app), disables hardware buttons, disables touch in circled regions or entirely, disables Motion (which locks rotation — useful since the manifest cannot), suppresses keyboards, and supports a time limit; it is started by triple-clicking the top button and ended with passcode or biometrics ([Apple Support HT202612](https://support.apple.com/en-us/ht202612)). `overscroll-behavior` is only partially supported on iOS Safari 16 through 27 ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_overscroll-behavior)), so the app shell should also be `position: fixed` with `overflow: hidden`. The remaining touch hygiene (`touch-action: manipulation`, `user-select: none`, `viewport-fit=cover` with safe-area insets) is long-standing practice that was not re-verified in this pass and should be checked on device. No framework or native wrapper is needed: none of the requirements — Web Audio, offline, IndexedDB, wake lock, Guided Access — requires Capacitor, and the only reason to wrap natively would be access to Siri-grade voices, which pre-recorded clips make moot. **Recommendation: vanilla JS ES modules on a free static HTTPS host (GitHub Pages, Netlify or Cloudflare Pages), hand-listed service-worker precache with a versioned cache name, no build step** (judgment call; no authoritative source prescribes a stack).

## A 10x9 classifier makes "10-family only" problem sets possible

Commercial generators cannot do what the Phase 2 skill tree needs. Soroban Dojo's worksheet studio exposes levels, operation families, digit bands and "drill focus" presets but does not explain how drills are classified by rule ([Soroban Dojo worksheets](https://dmoliveira.github.io/soroban-dojo/worksheets)); AbacusTrainer's generator offers only operation, rows 2–10 and question count ([AbacusTrainer generator](https://www.abacustrainer.com/work-sheet-generator)). The only open-source generator with any rule-restricting mechanism, `generate-soroban-training` (MIT, JS), does it indirectly by letting you exclude specific digit-pair "actions" such as `'8-4'` and `'1+4'` and by constraining running and final sums (`sub sum >= min`, `sub sum != zero`) ([GitHub webappbuilder/generate-soroban-training](https://github.com/webappbuilder/generate-soroban-training)). Its example exclusion list is precisely the set of pairs that need a 5-complement involving the digit 4 — the author hand-listed what a classifier should compute. The formal spec to port is Dyalog's APL rule set, which defines each rod as heaven bead h (0/1) and earth beads e (0–4), gives adding 5–9 as `h/e + d = (h+1)/e + ⌊d`, the carry as `p.(0/1.q) = (p+1).q`, and borrow propagation as `p.((0-1)/e-d) = (p-1).1/e - d`, with no negatives and the test case `0/0+7+7 -> 1.4` ([Dyalog dfns soroban](https://dfns.dyalog.com/eval_s_soroban.htm)). A teacher's write-up supplies the four pedagogical classes and the two carry exceptions: fives complement when "both numbers are less than five, and the rod doesn't have enough beads"; tens complement when the rod lacks beads and a carry is needed, with "a single ('ten') bead added on the next rod to its left"; combined when it is "ostensibly a tens complement but also needs a fives complement"; and when carrying into a left rod holding 4 "all of its beads are lowered," while a left rod holding 9 "is cleared and the single bead goes to the next rod to its left" ([Van Cleave](https://joevancleave.blogspot.com/2022/04/the-soroban-abacus-addition-method.html?m=1)).

The derived algorithm (an inference from those rules, to be unit-tested against a bead simulator before shipping) is short enough to include in full. For adding d to a rod showing v with no carry (v + d ≤ 9): if d ≥ 5 it is direct; if d < 5 and e + d ≤ 4 it is direct; otherwise it is a **five-complement** (set the five bead, remove 5 − d). With a carry (v + d ≥ 10), the rod must subtract 10 − d and the left rod gets +1; if that subtraction is direct the step is a **ten-complement**, and if it needs a five-complement the step is **combined**. Subtraction mirrors it. The combined addition cells on a single rod are exactly **5+6, 5+7, 5+8, 5+9, 6+6, 6+7, 6+8, 7+6, 7+7, 8+6**; the combined subtraction cells (units rod before borrow) are **1−6, 2−6, 3−6, 4−6, 2−7, 3−7, 4−7, 3−8, 4−8, 4−9**. Everything else with a carry or borrow is a plain ten-complement. Crucially, the +1 carry (or −1 borrow) on the left rod is itself a step to classify recursively: carrying into a 4 is a five-complement, carrying into a 9 cascades, borrowing from a 5 is a five-complement, borrowing from a 0 cascades. The whole-number simulator, not per-digit arithmetic, must be the source of truth: 49 + 1 must be rejected from a "five-complement only" set because the units move is a ten-complement, and 14 + 1 must be rejected from a "direct only" set because 4 + 1 on the units rod is a five-complement.

```js
const DIRECT=0, FIVE=1, TEN=2, COMBINED=3;
function classifyAdd(v, d) {            // v 0..9, d 1..9; class on THIS rod only
  const e = v % 5;
  if (v + d <= 9) return (d >= 5 || e + d <= 4) ? DIRECT : FIVE;
  return classifySubNoBorrow(v, 10 - d) === FIVE ? COMBINED : TEN;
}
function classifySubNoBorrow(v, d) {    // precondition v >= d
  return (d >= 5 || (v % 5) >= d) ? DIRECT : FIVE;
}
function classifySub(v, d) {
  if (v - d >= 0) return classifySubNoBorrow(v, d);
  const c = 10 - d, e = v % 5;
  return (c < 5 && e + c > 4) ? COMBINED : TEN;
}
function stepRules(total, delta) {       // Set of classes used across all rods, incl. carries/borrows
  const rules = new Set(), op = delta >= 0 ? 'add' : 'sub';
  const digits = String(Math.abs(delta)).split('').reverse().map(Number);
  const rods = String(total).padStart(12,'0').split('').reverse().map(Number);
  function applyDigit(i, d) {
    if (d === 0) return;
    const v = rods[i];
    if (op === 'add') { rules.add(classifyAdd(v, d));
      if (v + d >= 10) { rods[i] = v + d - 10; applyDigit(i + 1, 1); } else rods[i] = v + d; }
    else { rules.add(classifySub(v, d));
      if (v - d < 0) { rods[i] = v - d + 10; applyDigit(i + 1, 1); } else rods[i] = v - d; }
  }
  digits.forEach((d, i) => applyDigit(i, d));
  return rules;                          // e.g. {TEN, FIVE} for 49 + 1
}
```

The generator wraps this classifier in rejection sampling with a constructive fallback. Inputs are `allowedRules` (a subset of the four classes), `requiredRules` (at least one step must use one of these, so a "10-family" set actually exercises it), `maxTotal` (9, 99 or 999, enforced at every intermediate step so the rod count never exceeds the level), `minTotal = 0`, operand digit band, number of operands, and an `allowSubtraction` flag. For each operand slot, sample a signed delta within the band, require `0 ≤ total + delta ≤ maxTotal`, run `stepRules`, and accept only if the rule set is a subset of `allowedRules`; after N failed draws, enumerate every legal delta and pick uniformly (a 1–19 element scan for single-digit totals). Accept the finished problem only if the union of its step rules intersects `requiredRules`. Two constraints from `generate-soroban-training` are worth copying: never let a running total go negative, and optionally forbid a zero intermediate total mid-problem, since a cleared abacus confuses beginners ([GitHub](https://github.com/webappbuilder/generate-soroban-training)). For the very first subtraction levels, additionally forbid borrows entirely. Store the per-step rule set with each problem so the hint can say "this one is a small-friend move" and so review can target weak rules. The portal's 4–5 signed numbers per problem are simply repeated applications of this step generator with a running total. **Gap:** schools differ on whether adding 5–9 to a rod showing 0–4 is "direct" or a separate "direct with five bead" case, and on whether the combined move is taught as "−5 +x +10" or as a "+10 −x" shortcut; pick the naming her class used and keep it consistent.

## Big tap targets, obvious feedback, rewards for finishing not scoring

The UX evidence for 5–7-year-olds is thinner than the engineering evidence but consistent. Nielsen Norman's physical minimum is **1 cm x 1 cm per target with about 2 mm spacing**, larger for primary actions, and "young children need large, easy-to-reach controls because their physical skills are less developed" ([NN/g touch targets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/)). Sesame Workshop's preschool guidance says tapping is the most intuitive interaction, to avoid pinch and flick, to design for landscape because that is how preschoolers hold tablets, and to keep hotspots away from the bottom edge where resting wrists trigger accidental actions ([Cooney Center / Sesame Workshop](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/?p=19607)). A defensible keypad spec (inference) is 3x4 keys of 1.5–2.5 cm (roughly 60–96 CSS px), 8–10 px gutters, a large answer field directly above, placed lower-right but not flush to the bottom. An alternative worth offering is a "set the beads" answer mode on an on-screen soroban, which mirrors the physical one she owns.

Feedback must be exaggerated and errors prevented rather than reported. NN/g found children under 6 missed subtle feedback cues and recommends "exaggerated facial expressions or spoken statements" over subtle sounds, keeping required information on screen because working memory is small, and preventing errors where possible ([NN/g kids' cognition](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/kids-cognition/)). Children aged 6–8 can read but vocabulary matters — "submit" confused children, "start" or "go" worked — and minimal grey-and-white designs bore them ([UserTesting](https://www.usertesting.com/blog/ux-for-kids)). The Quick Math Jr. team learned that a 5-second tutorial video was largely forgotten while in-context instructions practiced immediately stuck, and that animation and music belong in menus, not during the task ([Shiny Things](https://medium.com/education-posts/making-apps-for-kids-part-1-8db62e374a68)). The resulting error pattern (inference): on a wrong answer, give an obvious but gentle visual and spoken cue, keep the problem and typed answer on screen, allow a second attempt, then show the correct answer with a bead animation for the specific rule that step needed — which the generator already knows. Disable keys that cannot be valid (no third digit when the maximum is 99), and give listening mode a replay button because audio-only information is lost to a small working memory.

Reward design should respect the motivation research. Resnick's Lifelong Kindergarten chapter summarizes Deci's and Lepper's findings that payments and "Good Player" certificates reduced later interest in an activity, but draws the key distinction that "gamification can work for training a specific skill on a set schedule," and notes Scratch deliberately has no points, badges or levels and caps counts at "100+" to discourage numeric competition ([MIT LCL Lifelong Kindergarten ch. 3](https://lcl.media.mit.edu/resources/readings/chapter3-gamification.pdf)). Daily soroban drill is exactly a narrow scheduled skill, so small rewards are acceptable if they are tied to effort rather than accuracy, non-competitive, and non-punitive: one completion sticker per day on a monthly calendar, no visible numeric scores in the child view, no streak that resets ("days practiced this month" instead), and mastery made visible through content — unlocking the next rule or a faster listening speed. Accuracy and rule-level statistics belong in the parent dashboard, consistent with Sesame's advice to provide parent tips and progress trackers ([Cooney Center](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/?p=19607)).

The parent gate follows Apple's Kids Category pattern: "adult-level tasks that must be completed in order to continue," with a spoken prompt for pre-readers ([Apple Kids Category](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/kids-apps/)); UserTesting's example is a child lock that "requires tapping a button two more times" ([UserTesting](https://www.usertesting.com/blog/ux-for-kids)), and NN/g shows cognitive limits used deliberately to keep kids out of settings ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/kids-cognition/)). For a home app the gate only needs to keep her in child mode: a low-contrast "For grown-ups" control away from the play area, press-and-hold, then a randomized adult task such as a two-digit multiplication rather than a fixed PIN that a 6-year-old watching over a shoulder will memorize (inference). Combine it with Guided Access for a shared iPad.

## The phased plan: replace, then teach, then go mental

**Phase 1 — drop-in replacement for practice (target: 2–3 weekends).** The goal is that on the day the class subscription ends, nothing about her practice ritual changes. Build the static PWA shell (manifest, `apple-touch-icon`, precache service worker, IndexedDB progress store, `persist()` on first run, export/import JSON), the classifier and generator with a unit-test suite seeded from Dyalog's cases, and the two drill screens in the portal's exact shape. Visual mode shows 4 signed single-digit numbers stacked vertically at once, keypad entry, 10 problems per set, a row of grey squares turning green or red, a visible session timer, and a wrong answer lets her move on and is re-queued after the tenth problem. Listening mode plays pre-recorded clips on the Web Audio clock with the inter-number gap as a slider in seconds, a replay button, a 3-second grace pause before the answer is requested, and a wake lock held during the drill. Parent mode is a hold-to-open gate exposing: rule set (default DIRECT + FIVE + TEN, COMBINED off), operand digits, operands per problem, interval seconds, problems per set, sound on/off, and a session log with missed problems and submitted versus correct answers. **Assumption:** the portal's current problem mix is single-digit operands with 10-family moves but without combined moves; confirm by inspecting a few of her recent portal sets before freezing defaults.

**Phase 2 — teach new rules with a skill tree (target: the following 1–2 months, shipped node by node).** The tree is the KSC/Dojo order rendered as unlockable nodes, each with an allowed-rule set, a required-rule set, a digit and operand band, and a gate. Nodes, in order: direct add; direct subtract; small-friend add (+1..+4 cases); small-friend subtract; big-friend add (pairs introduced one at a time, in whichever order her class used — the sources do not settle it); big-friend subtract; combined add (the 10 cells above); combined subtract (the 10 cells); 2-digit mixed x 5 rows; 3-digit mixed x 5 rows; then a "next band" placeholder for 10-row addition and 2x1 multiplication, which the Japanese norm places around month nine of year one ([Coeteco](https://coeteco.jp/articles/12704)). Parent mode grows a dashboard with per-rule accuracy over the last 20 problems, days practiced this month, and a one-screen "what to say" script per node drawn from the technique checklist above — the parent knows little soroban and needs the words, not just the stats. **Phase 3 — anzan (start only when Phase 2's big-friend nodes are automatic; expect this to be months away).** Add two small modes capped at 1–2 minutes a day. "Replay in your head" follows the practitioner recipe: after she solves a problem physically, the screen asks her to close her eyes and redo the same moves mentally, then checks the answer ([Art of Memory](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/anzan-flash-method/30070)). Flash anzan starts at the bottom of the official ladder — **1 digit, 2–3 numbers, 2.0–3.0 s per number, addition only, with a short blank between flashes** ([App Store 公式](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/%E3%83%95%E3%83%A9%E3%83%83%E3%82%B7%E3%83%A5%E6%9A%97%E7%AE%97-%E5%85%AC%E5%BC%8F/id421826349); [app-liv.jp](https://app-liv.jp/5354896/); [Soroban66](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1661734047)) — and advances by shortening time first, then adding numbers, then adding digits, with the ladder's own 5-problem promotion sets. Cap digits at 3 for the foreseeable future, since even expert mental-abacus users hold only three or four columns ([Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2011-08-stanford-explores-language.html)). Bead-to-number and number-to-bead flashcards are cheap to add and are the stabilizing exercise adult learners recommend ([Art of Memory](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/soroban-and-anzan/33294)). A listening-anzan variant (same clips, no abacus allowed) is a natural fourth mode once flash is comfortable. **Assumption:** 1-digit, 3-item, 1.5–2 s defaults for a child come from adult-learner and app-ladder evidence; no children's-program flash defaults were found.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the project from "build an abacus app" to "build a rule-aware problem generator and wrap it in three progressively thicker shells." The generator is where the home app can genuinely outdo both the paid portal and every commercial app surveyed, because none of them lets a parent say "big friends only, no combined moves, totals under 100" and get a set that actually exercises that rule — and that capability is exactly what turns a drill app into a teaching app when her class ends. Everything else is well-trodden: the drill shapes are standard exam shapes, the pacing ladders exist for flash anzan and can be borrowed, and the iPad platform now supports offline, large storage and wake locks for installed web apps without a native wrapper.

The pedagogy argues for restraint in two places developers usually over-build. Progression should be slow and accuracy-gated rather than gamified, because first-graders in the one controlled study struggled to reach expertise in a year and the kyu norm for a classroom child is only 9–8-kyu after twelve months; and anzan should stay a one-minute garnish until bead moves are automatic, because the mental representation is a fragile three-column visual image that physical fluency must build first. The parent, not the child, is the user who most needs the app's intelligence: the per-rule dashboard and the per-node script are what let someone who "knows a little soroban" keep a 6-year-old moving up the same ladder a Japanese teacher would use.
